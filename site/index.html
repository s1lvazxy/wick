02 The overheating question

We will not cook your laptop.

A closed laptop in a backpack is an oven with a keyboard. So Wick watches the heat the whole time, and gives up early rather than late. You can't turn this off. That's the feature.

Press and hold, with a mouse, a finger, or the space bar. Let go and it cools.

Awake, lid closed, perfectly comfortable.

The minute runs at 12× here. You have places to be.

  1. Hot with the lid closed, for a minute

    macOS reports a serious thermal state, or the battery reaches 45°C. Wick ends the session and the laptop sleeps. A single cooler reading starts the minute again.

  2. Critical, lid open or not

    On a Mac, Wick ends the session at once. No minute, no negotiation. On Linux it waits 30 seconds at the limit first, in case a sensor is lying.

  3. On Linux, the same idea

    It reads the kernel's own sensors. With the lid closed, a minute with the battery at 45°C, or with a sensor within 8°C of its critical point (5°C for the CPU's own), ends the session.

Still: keep it out of bags while it's awake. Wick can end a session. It can't air-condition a backpack.

03 Safety

It always puts sleep back.

Forgetting pmset disablesleep 1 is how a laptop ends up awake for a week. So Wick treats turning it off again as the main job.

  1. 1

    A guard outlives the app

    Every session is enforced by a small separate process. If the app crashes or the terminal closes, the timer still ends on time.

  2. 2

    Low battery stops it

    On battery, Wick lets go at 20% by default, so the laptop can sleep rather than die mid-task.

  3. 3

    Heat stops it

    See above. This is the one without a switch.

  4. 4

    Quitting, logging out or shutting down stops it

    Sleep comes back. If the guard is ever force-killed or the power cuts out, a background job picks the session up within five minutes.

  5. 5

    The permission is one line

    On a Mac, a sudoers rule that allows two exact commands and nothing else. Read it, or remove it with wick uninstall. Linux needs no root at all. 

    you ALL=(root) NOPASSWD: /usr/bin/pmset -a disablesleep 0, /usr/bin/pmset -a disablesleep 1

By morning, the work is done.

Fourteen steps out of fourteen. And the laptop sleeps like normal again.

04 Install

Four steps, about a minute.

On macOS 14 or later, Apple silicon or Intel. Or on Linux, with systemd.

  1. 01

    Get the app

    Homebrew is the easiest way to install and update Wick, and it adds the wick command too.

    brew install --cask s1lvazxy/tap/wick

    Update with brew upgrade --cask wick.

    Download from GitHub

    Unzip and drag Wick to Applications. Early builds aren't notarised, so the first time, open System Settings → Privacy & Security and choose Open Anyway.

    pipx install "git+https://github.com/s1lvazxy/wick#subdirectory=linux" && wick setup

    Adds a top bar extension on GNOME, or run wick app elsewhere. Needs systemd-logind, and no root at all, so step 3 below doesn't apply. There's also an RPM.

  2. 02

    Open it and press play

    Wick lives in your menu bar or top bar, with no window of its own. Click the mark, pick a duration, press play.

  3. 03

    Allow it, once

    On a Mac, only an admin can keep a closed Mac awake, so the first time, macOS asks for your password to add Wick's one-line rule. You won't be asked again. Linux skips this step.

  4. 04

    Close the lid

    That's it. Open it again whenever you like. The panel tells you how long is left, or why Wick stopped.

From the terminal

wick on 2h
Stay awake for two hours.
wick on --agents
Stay awake until your agents finish.
wick run claude
Stay awake while one command runs.
wick off
Back to normal, right now.

Questions

Will it overheat with the lid closed?

On a desk, rarely. In a bag, yes, and fast. Wick ends the session once the laptop has run hot with the lid closed for a minute, and at once if it turns critical. Keep it out of bags anyway.

Why not just use caffeinate?

caffeinate stops idle sleep, but closing the lid still puts the Mac to sleep. Only the disablesleep setting survives that, and it needs root.

What counts as an agent finishing?

Its process exiting. An agent left open at its prompt still counts as running, so use a timer or wick run if that's likely.

Do I need an external display or power?

Neither. It works on battery with nothing plugged in, and stops at the battery level you choose.

Does it work over SSH on Linux?

Only with a one-line polkit rule, because logind normally gives this lock to the person at the laptop. wick doctor prints the rule.

How do I remove it?

Run wick uninstall, then brew uninstall --cask wick or pipx uninstall wick-linux, or delete the app if you installed it by hand.
